# Campaign and dramatic contract

## Fixed story

Satan, bored with predictable cruelty, experiments with goodness as the cultured traveler Ian d’Au-Delà-Des-Monts. Tancrede is an adult bereaved prince. The murder of his family sends him toward the recovery of a kingdom whose people have reasons to distrust another king. Ondine, a princess he has seen in dreams, has a refugee corridor and her own political obligations to protect. The dreams are an intrusion she challenges, never evidence of entitlement or consent.

Ian cannot compel a freely made choice or restore the dead. He can change matter, but a lasting intervention must be accepted by someone who bears its consequences; spectacle also alerts the powers that police old bargains. His refusals follow these rules from chapter 1. Tancrede must do the investigative and moral work. Ian's early humor treats people as fascinating experiments; later he pays costs he no longer mentions. Do not explain the extinguished flame as a quantified kindness meter.

The regent Ysarn claims that centralized force will prevent another ruinous royal succession. He financed the murders but uses grain logistics, frightened town councils and soldiers' wages to hold power. Chancellor Alise wants courts and predictable taxation, not murder; Marshal Roul wants a winter his troops can survive. Their interests sometimes diverge. Exposure alone does not feed their dependents.

Ondine maps safe water routes, reads tidal and sluice systems, and will abandon Tancrede's timetable when it threatens refugees. She learns to share authority without surrendering her own purpose. Tancrede learns to accept a refusal, compensate losses and govern people who will never love him. Their romance develops through work, argument, apology, reciprocal rescue and finally a freely offered future.

## Ending to preserve verbatim in substance

Ian leaves after the kingdom is recovered. Tancrede never discovers that Ian is Satan. In the farewell, Tancrede searches the eyes that so often unsettled him: the bright flame is gone and the sadness seems without limit. Ian does not explain it. A small unfinished gesture, not a speech, closes their exchange.

Ian returns to Hell as Satan. Tancrede and Ondine reign together. Demons strangely leave the kingdom alone, as far as its rulers understand. No final reveal to Tancrede, no twist converting Ondine into a reward, no explanation of the flame in a journal entry or credits scene.

Provisional farewell exchange, to be performed and revised after the full journey:

Tancrede: “Will you come back?”

Ian: “You have people enough to disappoint you now.”

Tancrede almost smiles. Ian watches him for a moment too long, then straightens the clasp Tancrede never learned to fasten properly. His hand falls. Tancrede notices the absent light. He does not find a question for it.

Ondine: “The road is open.”

Ian: “Yes.”
